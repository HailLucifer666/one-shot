Our story

One grinder and a very patient landlord

How eleven seats, a one-group machine, and a lot of stubbornness became your favorite corner booth.

The Copper Cup opened in June 2016 with a one-group espresso machine, a single grinder, and a landlord who agreed to wait a month for the first rent check. We had eleven seats, one menu board, and a theory: if the coffee is honest and the room is warm, people come back.

The theory held. Regulars brought friends, friends brought laptops, laptops brought more regulars. In 2019 we knocked through the wall into the old bookshop next door, which is where the corner booth lives now. In 2022 we started roasting our own beans two blocks away, on a battered 1962 Probat we call Big Red.

We are still small on purpose. Rosa pulls the shots and trains the bar. Ben bakes before sunrise, which is why the croissants run out by ten. Everything else — the playlist, the plants, the dog — belongs to all of us.

“If the coffee is honest and the room is warm, people come back.”

What we stand on

Three things we will not budge on

Farms we can name

Every bean on the bar is bought direct from a producer we have met, at a price they set — usually two to three times the commodity rate. If we can’t name the farm, we don’t serve it.

Baked, not bought

The pastry case is empty when we close because nothing in it survived the day. Ben starts at four in the morning; the croissants land at seven and rarely see ten.

Room for everyone

Students, nurses coming off night shift, the Tuesday chess club, your book group. The booth is first-come, and it always will be.
